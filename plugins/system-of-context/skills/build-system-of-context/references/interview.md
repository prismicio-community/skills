# Interview

The interview closes what the harvest could not. Most of its value is in five
areas no website covers: claim vs reality, evidence restrictions, off-limits
topics, overloaded terms, and the offers table.

## Rules for asking

- Ask only what the harvest did not answer. Never ask the user to confirm
  something a strong internal source already states.
- Batch questions in groups of three or four. Use multiple-choice when the
  harvest produced candidates ("Your site says X. Is that fully true today?").
- "Don't know" is a valid answer. It becomes a gap with a named owner, not a
  guess.
- When the user's answer contradicts a source, use the user's answer, note the
  conflict, and ask who should fix the source.
- Show what you already have before asking for more. People correct faster
  than they write from scratch.
- Stop when the must-ask questions are answered. The rest can stay as gaps.

## Must ask

These protect the company from the costliest failures. Ask every one, even if
the answer is "none".

**Claim vs reality.**
"Which things does your positioning or website say that the product does not
fully do today? For example a feature that only works for one use case, a
category you claim but are not known for, an integration that is in beta."
Present any public claims the harvest flagged and ask about each.

**Off-limits topics.**
"Are there topics agents should never state or imply? Pricing, packaging,
roadmap, unreleased features, specific customers, legal or compliance claims?"
For pricing, ask where the line is: no figures at all, figures but no
discounts, or plan names allowed but not what each plan includes.

**Evidence restrictions.**
"Do any customer stories, logos or quotes have reuse limits? For example link
only, no social, no excerpts, internal only, or expired permission."
List every case study found and ask about each.

**Overloaded terms.**
"Does any word mean two different things inside the company? For example a
product named after a marketing tactic, or a team that uses 'motion' to mean
product line."

**Offers.**
Present the offers found. For each, ask: what signals mean an account is a
good fit, what signals mean it is not, and which proof point backs it.
This table is what downstream skills (such as personalized page generation)
use to decide what to sell to whom, so it must be explicit.

**Absolute style rules.**
"Any words, punctuation or formats that are banned outright?" Offer the
common ones as options: em dashes, exclamation marks, superlatives, a list of
AI-sounding words.

## Ask if the harvest is thin

**Positioning.** What are you, in one sentence? What category do buyers put
you in, and what category do you want to be in? What are you positioned
against: a competitor, a way of working, or doing nothing?

**ICP.** Who buys, who champions, who signs? What makes an account a great fit
regardless of size or industry? What size or industry patterns are real, and
how confident are you in them?

**Job to be done.** When a buyer reaches for you, what are they trying to get
done, and what happens if they don't?

**Competitive.** Who do you lose to most? What is the most common alternative
(often the status quo)? What is your honest angle against each?

**Win/loss.** Why do deals close? Why do they die? Is there a pattern by
segment or product? How confident is that read?

**Voice.** If the brand were a person, how would they talk? What does
on-brand sound like next to off-brand? Share an example of each if you have
one.

**Style.** American or British spelling? Serial comma? Heading case? How is
the company name written? How are product names capitalized?

## Ownership questions

Ask at the end, in one batch:

- Who owns positioning and messaging upstream? (They own compiled facts.)
- Which team owns learnings? (They write the Learnings page.)
- Where should the refresh skill look for updates: a Notion hub, a Drive
  folder, the website, or a scheduled re-interview?
- Who owns the System of Context as a whole, and approves refreshes?
- Where should it be saved: Notion, Google Drive, Confluence, project files, or somewhere else?

## Recording answers

Tag every answer `[interview]` in the scratch lists. If the user names a
document during the interview, read it and tag facts from it with the doc,
not the interview.
