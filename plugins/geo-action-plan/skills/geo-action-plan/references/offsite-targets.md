# Off-site mention targets

Read this during Step 6. AI assistants recommend brands largely from third-party consensus — listicles, reviews, forums — not from the brand's own site. On-site content is half the battle; this is the other half.

## The classification insight

**A publisher's willingness to mention the brand depends on whether the publisher's company competes in the brand's category — not on the article's topic.**

A reviews-platform vendor writing "best GEO tools" will happily add a CMS to the list: they gain completeness and lose nothing. A rival CMS writing the same article never will. Sorting targets by article topic wastes outreach cycles; sorting by ownership does not.

Classify from the domain's own site — fetch the homepage or about page when the company behind it is not obvious. Never classify from the article alone. When ownership is ambiguous, mark it **verify** rather than guessing; a misclassified competitor costs a full outreach cycle.

## Ownership classes

| Class | Definition (relative to the brand's category) | Posture |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent** | Media, affiliate sites, individual bloggers, newsletters, YouTubers with no product in any adjacent space | Highest priority — inclusion is their business model |
| **Non-competing vendor** | A company selling a different product category that publishes listicles and guides on these topics | High priority — the underused goldmine |
| **Adjacent vendor** | Different but overlapping or partnerable category | Medium — pitch as ecosystem or integration partner |
| **Direct competitor** | Same category as the brand | Excluded from pitching; listed separately as "unlikely" |
| **Review / aggregator** | G2, Capterra, Gartner Peer Insights, directories | Separate track: complete the profile, drive genuine reviews |
| **UGC / community** | Reddit, Quora, LinkedIn, YouTube, forums | Thread-engagement track, not pitching |

Confirm the direct-competitor list with the user before classifying. Supplement their list with brands that dominate the prompts in scope (from the AI visibility tool's brand data), and show the combined list for confirmation. Never silently decide who counts as a competitor.

## Ethics rules

These are non-negotiable, and they also make outreach more effective:

- **Thread comments genuinely answer the question**, disclose affiliation ("I work at X"), and add value a reader without the product still benefits from. No fake accounts, no vote manipulation, no undisclosed promotion. Beyond the ethics, communities detect and punish it, and Reddit's weight in AI answers makes a burned reputation expensive.
- **Pitches offer something real** — data, a free tool, an expert quote, a correction, or a genuinely missing entry with proof of fit. Never payment framed as editorial. If a site sells placements, record "paid placement available" as a fact for the user to decide on; never recommend disguising paid as earned.
- **Respect each community's self-promotion rules**, and note them per thread target when known.
- **Never invent a contact.** Record only what was actually found on the page or site — author byline, editor page, contact form. An invented email is worse than no contact route.

## Building and scoring the target list

Collect every third-party URL from both evidence layers with its metrics; exclude the brand's own domain; group by domain. Work down the evidence-strength ranking, classifying at minimum every domain appearing twice or more, or with above-median metrics.

For pitchable classes, check the specific cited or ranking page: article type; whether the brand is already mentioned (if yes, the ask changes to improving the description or position); a visible "last updated" date or update cadence, since recently-refreshed listicles accept additions and abandoned ones rarely do; and whether entries carry links.

For UGC URLs: platform, community, thread age, whether it still accepts comments, the question being asked, and any stated self-promotion rules.

**Priority** = evidence strength × inclusion likelihood × fit. A listicle with 22 retrievals is worth ten pitches to minor pages. Inclusion likelihood comes from the ownership class, update recency, and whether similar non-competing products already appear. Fit is whether the page's topic matches a prompt the brand needs to win.

Competitor-owned pages get no priority; they move to the "unlikely" list with one line on the only viable angles (integration announcement, data partnership).

## What each entry carries

**Pitchable target:** page URL · publisher domain · ownership class · the evidence metric with its layer ("cited 22× for [prompt]" / "ranks for 4 queries, w9") · **the ask**, one sentence ("add [product] to this list under [category]") · **the offer** (which brand asset makes it worth their while) · contact route, only if actually found.

**Thread target:** URL · platform and community · the question asked · evidence metrics · a **comment angle** of two or three sentences describing what a genuinely helpful, affiliation-disclosed comment would contribute, answer first and product second · community rules if known. Do not draft the full comment unless asked — the angle keeps it human.

**Review platform:** the profile URL · whether it exists and is complete · what is missing · whether competitors have more reviews and how many.

Cross-skill note: a publisher page the brand should also *beat* with its own content belongs in the content plan as well. Note the overlap in both tabs rather than duplicating the work.
